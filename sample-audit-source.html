Why these dates may differ from what you read elsewhere. The original Act put most duties at 2 August 2026. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the high-risk dates to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028. It did not move the bans, AI literacy, the general-purpose model rules or Article 50. A great deal of published guidance still shows the old dates.

5. Action plan

This month, because it is already law.

  1. Label the chatbot. One line of copy so students can tell they are talking to AI (Article 50(1)).
  2. Run one AI literacy session and keep the attendance list. Since July 2026 the duty is to take measures, not to guarantee a level per person, so a short session plus a record is proportionate proof (Article 4).
  3. Ask your chatbot vendor, in writing, whether its output is machine-readable marked. Keep the answer. It decides whether 2 December 2026 applies to you.

Next quarter, so December 2027 is not a scramble.

  1. Put real human oversight on the grader. A teacher sees the evidence before the score, can override it without friction, and the override rate is something you can report (Article 14).
  2. Turn on logging. Each score traceable to its inputs. Six months of retention is the floor your customers will have to meet (Articles 12 and 26(6)).
  3. Write the technical documentation. Purpose, training data, accuracy, limits, oversight (Annex IV). As an SME you may use the simplified form.

Before the deadline.

  1. Test for bias and write down the result across the student groups you actually serve (Article 10).
  2. Self-assess, declare, register. Education systems take the internal-control route, so no notified body is involved (Annex VI), then the declaration of conformity and the EU database entry (Articles 47 and 49).
  3. Re-check this report after any significant change to the grader, and at least once a year. A system already on the market is pulled into the new rules by significant design changes (Article 111).
What this report is not. It is an engineering and documentation map, not a legal opinion. Where a question turns on legal interpretation rather than on how the system is built, that is flagged in your report and a lawyer is brought in for sign-off.
